## Decision

**Gateling-TMS stops being pursued as a SaaS.** It stays a tool built for one academy, Mohamed's wife's online English academy. Mohamed chose this on 2026-09-25 (D10), after learning that Tamkeen already sells a superset of the product in Egypt.

## Problem Statement

Mohamed asked what is missing to sell Gateling-TMS, then asked the question underneath it: should this be a SaaS at all, or should he just build it for his wife's academy?

The code-level answer (billing, a paid reason, a student view, reminders) turned out to be the wrong layer. Nothing in the product stops a sale. What's missing is a buyer and a reason to pay, and the market check found an established local incumbent.

## Evidence

- **One real user:** Mohamed's wife runs an online English academy on Gateling daily. It replaced her scheduling and attendance chores.
- **Nobody has been asked for money,** including her.
- **No named outside buyer and no prospect list** (D2). She knows 2+ other academy owners (D3).
- **Segment size:** her academy and her peers each have **under 30 active students** (D4). The Free plan allows 50, so no limit ever triggers.
- **Current tool spend: nothing.** Free Meet/Zoom, WhatsApp, Sheets (D5).

## Landscape (researched 2026-09-25)

Web search summaries, not verified against vendor pages. Treat the numbers as rough.

- Small-tutor SaaS abroad is crowded and cheap: TutorBird $14.95/mo flat, TutorCruncher from £25/mo, plus pay-as-you-go plans aimed at tutors who won't subscribe ([Yo-Coach comparison](https://www.yo-coach.com/blog/best-tutor-management-software-compared/), [TutorCruncher PAYG](https://tutorcruncher.com/solutions/payg)).
- Solo-founder guidance (opinion pieces, not data): markets paying under ~$29/mo need volume that costs more to acquire than it earns ([Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/articles/best-micro-saas-ideas-solopreneurs)).
- **Closest local precedent:** Eduact (Cairo, 2020), SaaS for independent tutors and small centers across Egypt, including outside major cities, with video content, grading and **student-fee collection**. Abwaab acquired it in September 2026, citing the teacher-to-student trust network, not the software ([TechTimes, 2026-09-02](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/326352/20260902/abwaab-buys-eduact-jordans-edtech-reaches-egypts-students-through-their-teachers.htm)). The article gives no customer count or pricing.
- Western incumbents (Teach 'n Go from €69/mo, Teachworks, Tutorbase) don't target Arabic/RTL, EGP or WhatsApp-first academies (from the 2026-09-24 session, unverified).
- **Local Arabic competitors do exist, so "Arabic-first" is not a differentiator on its own.**
- **[Tamkeen](https://tamkeenapp.com/) (Egypt, since 2017) is a superset of Gateling v1.** It sells a TMS to Arabic-speaking training centers, academies and language institutes, with:
  - scheduling, attendance, and fees with installments and reminders
  - a student app for self-registration, payment, placement tests and choosing a group
  - live classes on Zoom, Teams and OnMeeting
  - sales/CRM, WhatsApp, QR certificates and AI grading of written answers
  - 50+ integrations, including Paymob, Fawry and Daftra

  It claims 1M+ students and **2.7B+ EGP in transactions**, so it already sits in the student-payment flow that option C was aiming for. There's no public price; you request a quote or start a free trial. (From the vendor's own site, not verified.)
- **The squeeze:** academies big enough to pay are Tamkeen's market, which sells through quotes. Small academies like the reference one use free tools. Gateling's only unclaimed space is small online academies that want free and self-serve, the segment this doc already shows won't pay.
- Also in the region: [Snatr (سناتر)](https://snatr.com/) is an Arabic platform for centers: teachers, students, multiple branches, financial reports. [Daftra](https://www.daftra.com/) sells a schools module in the region.
- **Build-your-own is also a status quo.** Ahmed Hassan, an academy owner Mohamed knows, built their own system that runs sales, operations and teaching together. When an owner builds their own, it means they felt the pain enough to pay for it with their own time. It also hints that the bundle serious academies want is **sales (leads → enrollment) + operations + teaching**. Gateling v1 has operations and teaching; sales is roadmap item 3 (CRM).

**Insight (superseded by the Tamkeen finding):** a seat subscription loses in the under-30-student segment, and the student-payment flow is where the money is. But Tamkeen already sits in that flow.

## Why not SaaS

1. **The segment that fits doesn't pay.** Small online academies (under 30 students, zero tool spend) are covered by Free forever and have no line item to replace.
2. **The segment that pays is taken.** Tamkeen has sold scheduling, attendance, fees, a student app, CRM, WhatsApp and payments to Egyptian academies and centers since 2017. It also already owns the "take a cut of student payments" position (2.7B+ EGP claimed).
3. **Selling means the harder half of the work.** Mohamed is a solo builder. A SaaS here needs sales, support and a differentiated position against a 9-year incumbent, on top of building.

## Approaches Considered

- **6-week paid-demand test with warm intros** (the earlier draft of this doc). Rejected: after the Tamkeen finding, the likely result didn't justify six weeks of selling. The reviewer concerns below refer to that rejected test.
- **Payments model (a cut of student fees).** Rejected: Tamkeen already runs it with Paymob and Fawry, and it's a regulated build.
- **Gate the test on "why aren't you on Tamkeen?"** Not taken. Mohamed decided directly.

## What "build it for her" means

- **Backlog = her requests only.** A feature ships when she asks for it and uses it. No speculative settings, modules or plans.
- **Freeze SaaS work.** No billing/Paymob, no paid modules (roadmap items 1-7), no pricing or plan-tier work, no landing-page growth work, no multi-academy features.
- **Don't rip anything out.** Multi-tenancy, the Free plan, the landing site and the owner plan-grant stay as they are. Removing them costs time and closes the door.
- **Fix public claims.** The landing site stays live, so the inflated academy figure (D153) and the "coming soon" paid tiers are statements to strangers. Remove the figure, and either hide the paid tiers or leave them clearly marked "coming soon". No new code beyond that.
- **Keep running costs minimal.** One academy needs no more than the current Vercel + Neon setup. Gemini and Google integrations stay only if she uses them.

## Reopen triggers

- Two or more owners in her network ask for it in the same term.

### R3-1 — completeness

**Problem**

> Nothing says what happens to academies that have already paid if week 6 ends in Pivot (rule 4) or Stop (rule 5). 'After a Stop outcome' only covers keeping Free. A payer was promised direct support from Mohamed within a working day plus term setup, and renewal is monthly. It isn't stated whether renewals keep being accepted, whether that support continues, whether the current month is refunded, or how the payer is told. Stop also says to 'build only what she asks for', which conflicts with an open-ended support promise to a paying outside academy.

**Remedy**

> Add a line for payers under Pivot and Stop. For example: stop accepting renewals, keep the paid support until the month already paid runs out, then move them to Free with notice. Or refund the unused part. Also say who tells them and when.

### R3-2 — consistency

**Problem**

> The week-1 script tells owners 'Free stays free; in week 4 I'll ask whether you'd pay to keep it'. But the week-4 ask was reworded to 'Would you pay 599.99 EGP a month for this, with me supporting you directly? Free stays free either way.' The phrase 'to keep it' still suggests they lose something if they don't pay, which is the wording R2-3 removed from the ask. Owners are primed with the old framing before the corrected ask.

**Remedy**

> Align the week-1 script with the ask, for example: 'Free stays free; in week 4 I'll ask whether you'd pay 599.99 a month for direct support from me.'

### R3-3 — consistency

**Problem**

> The Decision Rules heading says they apply at week 6, but rule 1 triggers 'at the end of week 1' and changes the schedule long before week 6. If rule 1 were only checked at week 6, an N < 2 test would run the full six weeks before it pauses and restarts.

**Remedy**

> Take rule 1 out of the week-6 list as a checkpoint at the end of week 1 (for example, add it to the week-1 row of the plan), or change the heading to say rule 1 is checked at the end of week 1 and rules 2-5 at week 6.

### R3-4 — consistency

**Problem**

> Rule 1 says 'the latest decision is week 9', but that ignores rule 3. After a rule-1 restart (week 1 + 2 weeks recruiting + a new 6-week run), a reprice round adds a re-ask in 'week 7' with payment due by 'the end of week 8'. The real latest decision is then about week 11. It also isn't said whether rule 3's 'week 7/8' counts from the original start or from the restart.

**Remedy**

> Say that week numbers after a restart count from the restarted week 1, and state the true latest decision week including a possible rule-3 round (about week 11 from the original start).

### R3-5 — clarity

**Problem**

> Rule 1 says to 'pause the original academies' schedule', but those academies were onboarded with real schedules and may already be running live classes on Gateling. It isn't clear whether 'pause' means stopping their use of the product or only pausing the test clock. It also isn't clear whether their usage during the recruiting weeks counts, and whether 'restart week 1 for everyone' means re-onboarding them or only resetting their week-2-4 usage window and observations.

**Remedy**

> State that the original academies keep using Gateling as normal, and that only the test calendar pauses. Say that their usage bar is measured on the restarted weeks 2-4, and that they aren't onboarded again.

### R3-6 — clarity

**Problem**

> The reason for leaving prices below 300 EGP out of rule 3 is not supported: 'below that, even 50 academies wouldn't cover the time spent supporting them'. The document gives no estimate of support hours per academy or of what Mohamed's time is worth. At 299 EGP, 50 academies would bring in about 15,000 EGP a month, so the claim can't be checked.

**Remedy**

> Add the rough support-time estimate and the hourly value behind the 300 EGP floor, or say the floor is a judgment call and not a calculated break-even.
